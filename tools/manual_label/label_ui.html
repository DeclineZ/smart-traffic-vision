Labeling rules

1. Every vehicle you can recognise as a vehicle gets a box: moving, queued, parked on the roadside or in a lot, far in the background. If you can tell it's a vehicle but not which kind, pick the most likely class (usually car). Skipping visible cars is the exact mistake we're fixing.
2. car (key 1): sedans, hatchbacks, taxis, SUVs/PPVs, all 1-ton pickups (Hilux, D-Max, Navara, Ranger, Triton), high-cage pickups (รถคอก), passenger vans (Commuter/HiAce), and pickup-based songthaews (red/blue/white songthaew built on a pickup).
3. truck (key 4): medium and heavy commercial trucks: 6-wheelers, 10-wheelers, 18-wheelers (one box for cab + trailer), box/delivery trucks with a separate cab, dump trucks, mixers, and songthaews built on a truck chassis (Isuzu Elf-type, dual rear wheels). Rule of thumb: dual rear wheels or a separate box body = truck, pickup bed = car.
4. bus (key 3): city buses, coaches, minibuses with bus bodies. Passenger vans are car.
5. motorcycle (key 2): one box around bike + rider(s) + anything carried. Parked motorcycles count. Bicycles: no box.
6. three_wheeler (key 5): tuk-tuks and salengs (motorcycle with a sidecar/cargo bucket). The box covers the whole thing.
7. Boxes are tight around the visible part of the vehicle. Mostly hidden behind another vehicle: box what you can see if it's at least ~25% visible.
8. Dashed boxes with ? are machine guesses the models disagreed on. Fix or confirm (C) each one. Solid boxes are still machine guesses: look at them all, and look for missing vehicles everywhere, especially small ones near the horizon and between big vehicles.
9. Ignore people, animals, bicycles, pushcarts and vehicles shown on billboards or reflections.
10. Bad frame (frozen, glitched, camera turned)? Skip frame with a note.
